# Generic 5055, 5065, and 6355 Motors

## Overview

Generic 50mm and 63mm outrunner motors represent the entry-level tier of the electric skateboard motor ecosystem. These motors, often sold under various brand names or unbranded, are manufactured by multiple OEMs in China and provide an affordable entry point for DIY builders. While quality can vary significantly between suppliers, these motors have enabled countless budget e-skate builds and serve as excellent learning platforms.

**Common Designations:** N5055, N5065, 5055, 5065, 6355, 6365  
**Primary Applications:** Budget electric skateboards, robotics, RC vehicles, light e-bikes

## Performance Characteristics

### Efficiency
- **Typical:** 80-85% (budget motors)
- **Better Units:** 85-88%
- **Comparison:** 3-5% lower than premium motors

### Heat Management
- **5055:** Limited thermal mass, overheats easily
- **5065:** Moderate thermal capacity
- **6355:** Better than smaller motors, still limited
- **Ventilation:** All open can for cooling

### Reliability Notes
**Advantages:**
- Low replacement cost
- Widely available
- Good for learning
- Acceptable for light use

**Concerns:**
- **Magnets:** Rarely epoxied from factory
- **Bearings:** Standard quality, not premium
- **Winding Quality:** Variable, often inconsistent
- **Hall Sensors:** When present, reliability varies
- **Shaft:** May not be hardened
- **Balance:** Variable, some have vibration

### Known Issues

#### Issue: Magnet Loosening
- **Frequency:** Common in high-mileage use
- **Solution:** Battle harden before use (highly recommended)
- **Symptoms:** Grinding, vibration, power loss

#### Issue: Bearing Wear
- **Cause:** Standard bearings, contamination
- **Solution:** Upgrade to sealed bearings if rebuilding
- **Prevention:** Keep motor clean and dry

#### Issue: Shaft Wear
- **Cause:** Soft shaft steel, set screw damage
- **Solution:** D-shaft pulleys, threadlocker
- **Prevention:** Regular fastener checks

#### Issue: Hall Sensor Failures (Sensored Versions)
- **Cause:** Poor quality sensors, wiring
- **Workaround:** Run sensorless
- **Solution:** Quality motors have better sensors

#### Issue: Inconsistent kV
- **Cause:** Manufacturing variance
- **Reality:** Generic motors often ±10% kV tolerance
- **Impact:** Dual motor setups may need individual VESC tuning

## When to Choose Generic Motors

### Good Choice When:
- ✅ Budget is primary concern
- ✅ First build for learning
- ✅ Light rider weight (<150 lbs)
- ✅ Flat terrain, no hills
- ✅ Testing and experimentation
- ✅ Robotics, non-e-skate applications
- ✅ Short commute, low speed

### Consider Upgrading When:
- ❌ Rider weight >180 lbs
- ❌ Steep hills required
- ❌ High speed needs (>35 km/h)
- ❌ Long-distance reliability needed
- ❌ Aggressive riding style
- ❌ Commercial/revenue use

## Maintenance Tips

### New Motor Preparation
1. **Battle Harden:** Epoxy magnets before first use
2. **Check Fasteners:** Verify all bolts tight
3. **Balance Check:** Spin motor, check for vibration
4. **VESC Detection:** Run full motor detection
5. **Thermal Limit:** Set conservative 80°C limit

### Ongoing Maintenance
- Check pulley set screws monthly
- Clean dust/debris regularly
- Monitor bearing noise
- Watch for magnet noise
- Keep connections secure

## Summary

Generic 5055/5065/6355 motors are the gateway to DIY electric skateboarding. While they lack the refinement of premium motors, they offer:
- **Accessibility:** Low cost enables entry into the hobby
- **Learning:** Mistakes are affordable
- **Adequate Performance:** Suitable for many use cases

**Key Recommendations:**
1. Always battle harden generic motors
2. Run sensorless if hall sensors are questionable
3. Set conservative thermal limits in VESC
4. Consider upgrade path in your build planning
5. Check reviews for specific seller quality
