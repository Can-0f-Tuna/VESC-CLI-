# Turnigy Aerodrive SK3 and SK8 Motors

## Overview

Turnigy is HobbyKing's house brand, offering affordable brushless motors for RC and DIY electric vehicle applications. The Aerodrive SK3 series is a popular budget option for electric skateboards, while the SK8 series adds hall sensors for sensored operation. These motors provide good performance at a lower price point, making them accessible to budget-conscious builders, though they may lack some features of premium motors.

**Manufacturer:** Turnigy (HobbyKing brand)  
**Official Website:** https://hobbyking.com  
**Primary Applications:** RC aircraft, electric skateboards (DIY), e-bikes, robotics

## Construction Features

### Quality Components
- **Sintered Neodymium Magnets:** Strongest commercially available permanent magnets
- **Double Shielded Bearings:** Better contamination resistance than metal shielded
- **String Wound Stator:** Consistent winding accuracy
- **High-Quality Finish:** Anodized aluminum can

### Shaft Design
- **Removable Shaft:** Can be removed for keyway machining (see community guides)
- **Grub Screw Retention:** Two set screws on can end
- **Circlip Retention:** Shaft held by circlip on output end
- **No Keyway (Stock):** Shaft is smooth 8mm cylinder

### Mounting Considerations
**Important:** SK3 6374 motors have had different mounting patterns over production batches:
- Early versions: 32mm hole spacing
- Later versions: 44mm hole spacing
- **Action Required:** Verify mounting pattern before purchasing motor mounts

## Performance Characteristics

### Efficiency
- **Good for Price Point:** Budget motor efficiency
- **No-Load Current:** Moderate (bearings not as premium as expensive motors)
- **Stator Quality:** String wound, not as consistent as precision-wound premium motors

### Heat Management
- **Open Can Design:** Good airflow, but magnets exposed
- **No Thermal Protection:** No built-in temperature sensor (SK3)
- **SK8 Version:** May have basic temp sensing

### Reliability Notes
**Advantages:**
- Affordable replacement cost
- Widely available
- Good community support

**Concerns:**
- **Magnets Not Epoxied:** Factory magnets may loosen with high temperature/high mileage
- **Bearings:** Good but not premium grade
- **Shaft Set Screws:** Can loosen; threadlocker recommended
- **Hall Sensors (SK8):** Quality varies; some reports of early failures

### Known Issues

#### Issue: Magnet Loosening
- **Cause:** No factory epoxy on magnets (SK3)
- **Symptoms:** Grinding noise, vibration, power loss
- **Solution:** Battle harden (epoxy magnets) before use
- **Prevention:** Monitor temperature, avoid sustained high loads

#### Issue: Shaft Slippage
- **Cause:** Set screws loosening
- **Symptoms:** Pulley spins on shaft
- **Solution:** Use threadlocker, check regularly
- **Prevention:** D-shaft modification or better pulley retention

#### Issue: Mounting Pattern Variations
- **Cause:** Manufacturing changes over time
- **Symptoms:** Motor doesn't fit standard mounts
- **Solution:** Verify 32mm vs 44mm before purchase
- **Workaround:** Custom adapter plates

## Modification Guide

### Adding Battle Hardening
1. Remove motor can (careful - magnets are strong!)
2. Clean magnet surfaces
3. Apply high-temp epoxy between magnets
4. Let cure fully before reassembly
5. Balance if necessary

### Machining Shaft Keyway
See RC Universe forum guide:
1. Remove circlip and washer from shaft end
2. Remove grub screws from can end
3. Slide shaft out (armature will be loose - support it)
4. Machine 3x3mm keyway
5. Reassemble with proper alignment

### D-Shaft Conversion
- Alternative to keyway
- Grind flat on shaft
- Use pulley with set screw on flat
- More secure than keyway for high torque

## Important Notes

⚠️ **Caution:** Turnigy SK3 motors are RC aircraft motors, not specifically designed for electric skateboards. While they work well, they lack:
- Pre-epoxied magnets
- Premium bearings
- Skateboard-specific shaft features
- Temperature sensors (SK3)

**Recommendations for E-Skate Use:**
1. Battle harden before installation
2. Use threadlocker on all fasteners
3. Add temperature monitoring
4. Don't exceed 80A continuous per motor
5. Verify mounting pattern compatibility
